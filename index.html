How the rating works

Five steps from ball to board

Built on 200,961 deliveries from 867 IPL and T20I matches, 2022 to 2026. The full method, tests and sources are in the report.

01

How much does the ball matter?

Before every ball a model estimates the batting side's chance of winning. A ball matters when a four, a dot or a wicket would swing it. The last ball of the 2023 IPL final mattered 14.6 times as much as an average ball.

02

Five kinds of pressure

The scoreboard is one source. Big matches, walking in during a collapse, being hit for boundaries, a squeeze of dot balls and trailing in the game are the others. A new batter arriving in a crisis scores 14 runs per 100 balls slower.

03

Against an average player

Every ball is compared with what an average player does in the same spot: same over, wickets, target, ground and type of bowler, adjusted for the strength of the opposition. IPL and international cricket then sit in one table.

04

Three questions

How good on pressure balls, how often the player comes through, and how often it goes badly wrong. Each counts for as much as it proved repeatable in the data. Short records are pulled towards average.

05

Try to break it

200 rebuilds on different mixes of matches, two halves of the data rated apart, and a test of whether 2022–24 predicts 2025–26. It does for bowlers. It does not for batters, and the report says so.

Biggest finding

There is no clutch gene

Once luck is allowed for, no player is reliably better under pressure than their own usual standard. The players who deliver are good players who don't get worse, so the ratings reward doing it match after match.
